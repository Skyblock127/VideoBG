VideoBG 1.1 - video wallpaper and desktop clock for Windows 10/11 (64-bit)
==========================================================================

What it does
  - Plays a video behind your desktop icons. Ctrl+Alt+B turns it on and off;
    when it's off the video part isn't running at all (no CPU, ~3 MB RAM).
  - Shows a clock on the desktop (day, date and time), with its own place,
    colour, size and glow for the video wallpaper and for your normal
    wallpaper. It comes and goes with VideoBG.

Install (no admin rights needed)
  1. Right-click the zip > Extract All. Put the extracted VideoBG folder
     where it can stay, for example C:\Tools\VideoBG (not in Downloads if
     you clean that out: VideoBG runs from this folder).
  2. Double-click Install.cmd. It adds VideoBG to the Start menu and to your
     startup apps, and opens VideoBG.
     If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click "More info" >
     "Run anyway" (the app isn't code-signed, that's all).
  3. On the Video page: Choose video, top right (MP4, MOV, MKV, WebM,
     AVI...). An animated GIF works too: VideoBG turns it into a video once.
  VideoBG then lives in the tray (notification area): click its icon, or
  open VideoBG from the Start menu, to get the settings back.

Using it
  - Ctrl+Alt+B: wallpaper on/off (change the keys on the General page).
  - VideoBG starts the way you left it (on or off); change that on the
    General page ("When app starts").
  - Video page: "Crop" (on the preview) picks the part of the video shown.
    "Versions" (next to the video's details) lists smaller copies of it
    (your screen's size, 900p, 720p...) with the memory each takes: click
    one to see it on your desktop first, then "Make it" (a few seconds) or
    "Use it" to switch to one you made before. The original isn't touched.
  - While the wallpaper is paused for a while (PC locked, screen off, or
    covered by apps if you chose that on the Power saving page), VideoBG
    lets go of the video's memory and picks up at the same frame later.
  - Show on lock screen (switch under the preview, on by default): while
    the video wallpaper is on, the lock screen and the Windows desktop
    background (seen behind admin prompts and at sign-in) show a frame of
    the video (pick it with the Preview slider); while it's off, your own
    pictures. Exiting VideoBG from the tray puts your own pictures back.
    (The desktop background only follows with one screen and a
    single-picture background on Windows 11 24H2 or newer.)
  - Desktop clock page:
      * Video wallpaper / Still wallpaper picks which look you're editing.
        "Just for this video" gives the current video its own look.
      * Drag the clock on the preview to place it (it snaps to the middle;
        hold Alt to place it freely, arrow keys nudge it).
      * Colour: swatches; Pick takes a colour from the wallpaper; More opens
        the colour dialog (colour field, hex codes, and 16 boxes for your own
        colours: click a box, then "Save to box"; double-click a box to use
        it). Ctrl+V pastes a colour code like #72ABDE.
      * Size, opacity, glow (strength, size and colour) and 12/24-hour time.
      * The day name uses the Anurati font of the original Mond clock if you
        install it (free for personal use, so it isn't included: search for
        "Anurati font", right-click the file > Install). Until then it's
        written in Quicksand.
  - Sound, Playback and Power saving pages: sound source, speed, frame rate,
    and pausing while apps cover the desktop or on battery.
  - Start with Windows: turn it on/off in Windows Settings > Apps > Startup.

If a video won't play
  VideoBG says why on the Video page (and in a notification), with the fix.
  Most often Windows needs a codec extension for it. AV1 videos (common in
  4K downloads from YouTube) need the free "AV1 Video Extension": click
  "Get it free", install it from Microsoft Store, come back to VideoBG and
  the wallpaper starts by itself. VP9 and MPEG-2 have free extensions too;
  HEVC (H.265) needs "HEVC Video Extensions" (small fee).

Update (no need to uninstall first)
  Tray icon > Exit, extract the new zip into the same folder (choose
  "Replace the files"), then double-click Install.cmd again. Your settings
  are kept.

Remove
  Double-click Uninstall.cmd in the VideoBG folder (it puts your own pictures
  back and asks whether to delete your settings too), then
  delete the folder.

Settings live in %APPDATA%\VideoBG, video versions in %LOCALAPPDATA%\VideoBG.
The Quicksand font built into VideoBG is under the SIL Open Font License
(Quicksand font license.txt).
